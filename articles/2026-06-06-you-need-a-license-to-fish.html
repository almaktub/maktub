You Need a License to Fish

But Not to Raise a Human Being
Published June 6, 2026

There I was, doing what every person with too many thoughts and not enough sleep does at night: scrolling. And there she was. Honey Boo Boo. Apparently pregnant. Taking shots with a group of people I did not recognize.

I put my phone down and stared at nothing for a while.

Her choices are her business, and I have no place in that conversation. But the image did something to my brain I couldn't undo. You need a license to drive a car. To fish. To cut hair. To sell a house. You need a background check to coach Little League.

There's no background check to create a human being.

You can wake up one morning, make a decision you barely thought through, and nine months later be in charge of the entire inner architecture of a person who will go on to deal with the rest of us. Nobody checks whether you can manage your own emotions before handing you someone else's.

The DMV line for my driver's license was forty-five minutes long. There was a written test, a vision check, and a driving exam with a stranger in the passenger seat watching my hands. Then a probation period before I was allowed to drive at night.

And those same bureaucrats, the ones who wouldn't let me merge without signaling, had absolutely nothing to say about the other thing.

I'm not naive about why. The second anyone tries to build a gate around reproduction, history shows up with a body count. Every time someone has proposed "standards" for who gets to have children, it ended up aimed at the wrong people for the wrong reasons: the poor, the disabled, certain races, whoever the people in charge found inconvenient. I know. I'm not proposing a system or writing policy.

I'm pointing out that we never even have the conversation.

We built courts and contracts and health codes and traffic laws and food safety standards because we decided, correctly, that some things are too consequential to leave to chance. Then we looked at the single most consequential thing a person can do, putting someone into the world and shaping who they become, and collectively decided to wing it.

You have to pass a test to be a substitute teacher. You don't have to pass anything to become someone's entire childhood.

Maybe Honey Boo Boo will be a wonderful mother. People surprise you. Plenty of people with no business raising anyone do it with more tenderness than the ones who planned everything, and plenty of people with every resource and the best intentions still leave their kids in therapy for a decade. Maybe a license wouldn't fix it. Maybe you can't test for what actually matters: patience, consistency, showing up on the days you've got nothing left.

But I'd like us to at least admit the gap. We treat driving a car as worth gatekeeping and treat raising a human being as something that needs no thought before it starts. The hardest, most permanent job there is, the one whose output we all have to live with, is the only one with no application.

We are very serious about the infrastructure of civilization. We have never once asked whether you are prepared to produce the people who run it.
