The Asterisk

July 15, 2026 · 9:41 PM

Nobody in the history of this sport carries an asterisk the size of Lionel Messi's, and I want to get that out of the way before anyone thinks this is going somewhere else. He might be your goat. Fine. Genuinely, fine. This is my website, that's my opinion, and if the two of those together bother you, the internet is enormous and mostly full of people who'll agree with you instead.

Here's the case, condensed from a rant I've apparently been giving to anyone who'll sit still for it. Argentina cakewalked through this World Cup on a path softer than it had any business being, and every close call along the way seemed to land on their side of the coin. That's not new. It happened in 2022 too. I don't think Messi personally rigged anything, I think it's simpler and worse than that. I think football's biggest institutions decided a while back that the sport needed one more storybook ending before he retired, and they've been quietly clearing the runway ever since. Is he talented? Obviously. Nobody's arguing the man can't play. I'm arguing he's the only player in the history of the sport who's never once had to walk it alone.

Which is how I ended up, this week, in the strange position of praying for Barcelona. I don't say that lightly. Barcelona plays the kind of football that makes me want to lie down. But Lamine Yamal is the one exception that club has ever produced that I'd actually get out of bed for, a teenager from a rough part of a rough city, Muslim, poor before he was anything else, who has quietly been the best player on the pitch more nights than not since he was seventeen. I would rather watch a kid who had to be good to get out, beat a man who's never had to be anything but good, because somebody's been holding the door for him his entire career.

There's a version of Messi's life off the pitch that bothers me just as much as the one on it. The photo ops with royals and heads of state who wouldn't grant his own family a visa if the cameras weren't rolling. The way controversy just slides off him, never sticking the way it sticks to literally everyone else who's ever been photographed next to the wrong person for a paycheck. I can't prove intent. I can only tell you what it looks like from where I'm sitting, which is a man who's been protected by every institution he's ever needed protecting from.

All of that is the part I could say out loud at a dinner table without anyone flinching. Here's the part I wasn't planning on writing.

The day of the semifinal I had the worst reaction to a soccer match of my entire life. Not the worst reaction to a bad result. The worst reaction, period. Worse than anything I remember feeling the morning after the one real breakup I've had. Work had already been the kind of shitty that grinds you down hours before kickoff, so by the time the match started I wasn't exactly walking in fresh. I sat there so wound up that I ended up canceling a doctor's appointment, because I genuinely didn't trust myself not to say something rude to a person who'd done nothing to me. It was only later, on the phone with the cancellation line, that I remembered I hadn't taken my medication that day. I don't actually know if that's the real explanation or just the convenient one, or if it was just a bad day stacked on a bad day that happened to end with a soccer match. Maybe all three. None of it's flattering.

Either way, that's the part that actually scared me, more than any of the football did. I'd been saying for months that this exact final was coming, that the whole tournament was built for it, so some part of me clearly saw it a mile off. And I still didn't see it coming for me. I drove home with the radio on, some song I couldn't have named if you'd asked, not registering that I was even mad about anything specific, just driving. Then it hit me all at once, like someone flipped a switch, what I'd actually been carrying all night. I took one deep breath in the driver's seat and the whole thing cracked open.

I don't think I was actually that upset about a football match. I think the football match was just the first thing all day that gave the feeling somewhere to go.

I still want Lamine Yamal to win. I still think Messi's career should come stapled to an asterisk nobody bothers reading anymore.

But some nights I wonder how much of that hatred was really about him, and how much of it was just easier to spend on a stranger in Argentina than on myself.
