The Greatest Band of All Time

June 18, 2026

The Beatles are the greatest band of all time. Not my opinion. Fact. Four guys from Liverpool who somehow wrote a song for every single emotion a human being is capable of feeling. Heartbreak, joy, longing, confusion, peace, grief, the specific feeling of a Sunday afternoon when you don't know what to do with yourself. They have a song for that. They have a song for all of it. Sixty years later, every generation finds them and thinks they discovered something personal. That is not an accident. That is the whole point. And if you need an example of what I mean, here you go, buffoons.

I didn't see them coming. One second I'm standing at the edge of La Paz Park, waiting for guests to arrive, and the next I'm on my back in the grass with three kids on top of me. Deen, Jenna, and Jasmine. My cousin B's kids. Deen is in kindergarten. Jenna's nickname is Guddi, which means Doll, because she looks exactly like one and everyone knows it. Jasmine can't talk yet. None of that stopped them from charging at me like I'd stolen something from them and they wanted it back. I lay there for a second, looking up at the sky, and I felt something I didn't have a name for.

We were there for my brother's graduation. Enough people that you could lose yourself in the crowd if you wanted to. I was doing exactly that, standing at the edge of things, when I saw B walk in with the kids. Normally at these things they get swallowed up by the noise. There's always someone more interesting than you. But all three of them saw me at the same time, and something happened in their faces, and then they were running.

I didn't have time to brace.

I've known B my whole life. She's family in the specific way that means you don't have to explain yourself, except that I had spent a few years giving her plenty to need explained. An ex. A version of myself I don't fully recognize now. A slow drift away from everyone I actually loved, pulled by something I mistook for something else. I missed two of her kids being born. I told myself reasons at the time. They were the kind of reasons that only hold up if you don't look at them too long.

B forgave me without making it a production. She didn't ask for an accounting. She just let me back in, brought her kids with her, and acted like the door had always been open. That's a particular kind of grace, the kind that doesn't wait for you to deserve it.

But I don't think I'd actually received it until that afternoon in the grass.

There's something about being loved by a child that has no patience for your defenses. They don't come at you with context or history. They don't weigh what you did or didn't do. Jasmine can't even talk and she still made it across that field. You can't meet that with anything other than what it is. And what it is, is love. Unfiltered, unearned, with no strings attached and no memory of the times you didn't show up.

When it landed, I realized something I hadn't been aware of. I'd been letting love out for years, directing it at people, spending it freely, but I had built something on the receiving end without knowing it. Not exactly a wall. More like a habit of deflection. Of letting good things arrive and then quietly redirecting them before they could settle. You spend long enough in something that makes you feel small, and even after you leave it, the posture stays. The bracing. The instinct to not quite let the good things fully in.

Three kids in a park dismantled years of that in about four seconds.

I lay in the grass longer than I had any reason to. They climbed off eventually, pulled toward something shinier the way kids always are, and I sat up and stayed there. The graduation was happening somewhere behind me. People were arriving, finding seats, living their afternoon. None of it felt as immediate as that patch of ground.

Later, replaying the moment, the only thing that kept surfacing was the last line the Beatles ever wrote. And in the end, the love you take is equal to the love you make. I'd carried that line around for years. Thought I understood it. Thought it was about output, about putting love into the world and trusting it would return. A kind of cosmic balance sheet.

But in that grass, it meant something different. The love you take. Not make. Take. Receive it. Let it in. Actually open the door on your end instead of just pushing things out through yours and wondering why the ledger never feels even. I'd been making plenty of love for a long time. I just hadn't been taking any. And the equation only works if both sides are open.

I don't know if Deen will ever understand what he did for me. I don't know if any of them will. But when someone who has a reason to be cautious around you, because you weren't there, because you missed the beginning of their life, when that person still runs at you like you're exactly where you belong, something either breaks or opens.

I'm still figuring out which one it was.

Maybe it's the same thing.
