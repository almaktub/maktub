The Square

Published October 2, 2023

You've seen the square.

Black background, white text. PRAY FOR [CITY]. Sometimes a flag. Sometimes a candle emoji. Forty-seven likes, a few hearts, one person in the comments saying "stay strong." You scroll past it, or share it, or make one yourself.

The city keeps burning.

I used to think awareness was the obstacle. If people really knew what was happening, something would shift. The gap between atrocity and response was an information gap, and the fix was documentation.

Then we got the documentation.

Live footage. Phone video from inside the thing while it's happening. You can watch a city get bombed and read posts from the people under it before the dust settles. The information gap is gone.

The city keeps burning.

The old story went: people don't act because they don't know, so show them, make it undeniable, and they'll move. Now there's a version of the world where everyone knows and the knowing has nothing to do with the outcome. Awareness floats over the event like a balloon somebody let go of. Visible, drifting, doing nothing to stop the fall.

Part of it is the scroll. A person can only hold so much outrage before the body starts numbing to it. This city today, that one tomorrow, a different atrocity next week, and somewhere along the way the brain stops treating it as an emergency and starts treating it as weather. Something that happens. Something you post about. Something that passes.

And everyone's in their hamster wheel. You have rent, you have kids, you have a job eating you alive, and somewhere behind all of it is a war that started before your morning coffee. It doesn't stop being real because you can't fix it. It doesn't stop because you posted the square either.

I don't think the square is worthless. I just think we oversold it. We thought information was power. It turns out information is just information. Power is somewhere else, harder to find, and it doesn't live in a share button.

So we make a square and move on, the city keeps burning, and tomorrow there's a new square and we do the whole thing again.

The square was never supposed to be the thing you did. It was supposed to be the reason you did something else.
