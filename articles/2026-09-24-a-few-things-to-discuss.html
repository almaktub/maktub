A Few Things to Discuss

Published September 24, 2026

I was driving home from work with "Blue" by Little Simz on when she said something I couldn't shake for the rest of the drive. God chose her for the job, and she agreed.

The agreeing is what got me. I'm much faster at agreeing that something is my fault. Hand me a bad situation and I'll work it backward, looking for the turn I should have taken, the thing I should have caught. Sometimes that's useful. Mostly I'm confessing to things I wasn't even in the room for.

She made me think about my sister. About my uncle, who became my best friend after we lost her, until we lost him too. And about my father.

My father lived through the Soviet invasion. When the mujahideen took over, he and my mother left for Pakistan, which is where I was born. When Karzai took over he moved us all back to Kabul, because the Taliban were gone and you go home when home is free. Inside Kabul we moved again and again. In 2014 he decided there was nowhere left in the country to move to, and started over one more time, in America.

In Kabul he worked as a translator and a travel expeditor. He was the man who could talk to both sides of the room and get people where they needed to be. Here, he works private security. He likes it. I'm not writing this to take that away from him. But I know what he's capable of, and I've watched life shrink him to fit whatever space was left over.

I wonder how many times he looked up and thought, surely this is enough now. Surely I've paid.

I've had my own version of that conversation. Why this. Why again. What exactly am I supposed to do with this one.

I don't think God makes mistakes. Not many, anyway. We have a few things to discuss, but that's between me and him.

I do think he picks people for things. I know how that sounds when the thing is someone you love dying. I would have happily been the wrong guy for that job. Nobody wants to find out how much grief they can carry by being handed more.

But in the car I noticed something. When you're inside something terrible, you don't get to know how you'll talk about it later. You have whatever you have that day, and some days it isn't much. Then years go by, and one afternoon you hear yourself telling the story. You lived long enough for there to be an after.

My father keeps me honest about what that after looks like. Getting through it didn't mean getting through it whole. He gave things he isn't getting back. So did I.

My sister is still gone. My uncle is still gone. There's no version of being strong enough that fixes either of those sentences.

Where I've been unfair to myself is expecting strength to feel like strength while it's happening. It never has. Every time, I felt like the wrong person for it, and every time I got through it anyway. The feeling and the record don't match, and I keep trusting the feeling.

If I believe God knows what he's doing, I can give a little of that faith to his opinion of me.

I'm not volunteering for anything else. My family and I would love a quiet stretch.

But next time that line comes around, I'm singing it.

God said I'm the best for the joooooooooooooob, I agree!
