Get Your Money Right First

Published August 2, 2026

Four years since my last relationship ended, and I still haven't tried again. Ask me why and I've got an answer loaded and ready: I need to get my money right first. Sometimes I upgrade it to mental health, say I'm working on myself before I put that weight on someone else. Both sound responsible. Both are also, if I'm honest, a nicer way of saying I'm scared, and "not ready yet" reads a lot better in a text than "terrified, please don't leave me on read."

I used to believe my own excuse completely. Then I started noticing everyone else running the same script with different props.

Get your money right before you go looking for love, we say, like it's gospel. Meanwhile there are homeless people sleeping curled into each other under an overpass every night this winter, and nobody calls that love, because it's dirty, because it's beneath the version of love we've agreed to respect. Somebody decided love needs collateral. I'd like to know who. It wasn't anyone actually broke, because broke people seem to be managing fine without a savings account.

My grandparents' generation had six or seven kids sometimes, and somehow all six or seven got loved like they were the only one. Now it's one kid cycling through a stepfather, a mother's new boyfriend, a father's second family two states over, each one introduced with the same forced smile. Sometimes leaving is exactly the right call. But we didn't run out of love. We ran out of staying.

I know my part in that. Therapy language taught me to call avoidance "protecting my peace," which sounds like wisdom and is really just being scared with better lighting. I've said "I'm not in the right headspace" to my own reflection more times than I've said it to another person, and both times I meant "I'm scared, and this sentence is shorter."

I don't think I'm uniquely broken. I think I'm early-onset normal. Ask anyone my age who hasn't dated in years why not, and count how many times you hear "the timing" before someone finally says "I got hurt and never figured out how to risk it again." Timing is the socially acceptable translation. It always has been.

Four years is a long time to hide behind a good-sounding sentence. Long enough that I finally noticed the sentence was the problem, not the excuse for it. My money, for the record, is fine. Has been for a while. I just check the balance every so often like it's going to say something different this time.

I'm not fixing this in one essay, and I'm not pretending the fix is easy: text someone, take the leap, cue the movie score. That isn't how fear works, mine or anyone else's. But I can at least stop calling it a budgeting issue, a mental health issue, a "when the time is right" issue. It was never any of those. What I've actually been waiting for is the day I can't hear myself lie anymore.

Maybe there isn't somebody out there for all eight billion of us. Maybe some of us push the boulder up and there's nothing waiting at the top.

I'm pushing anyway.
