Without the Loudspeaker

Published September 24, 2026

The march started at Balboa Park and ran down through the Gaslamp to the court building by the docks. Somewhere along the way, someone tried to hand me a loudspeaker. I turned it down. My voice carries fine on its own, which anyone who has ever lived with me can unfortunately confirm.

I wanted to lose it that day. I wanted to go home hoarse.

I'd seen the video at work. I locked my computer to go to lunch and found my coworkers all on their phones, scrolling fast, holding their screens out to each other. I figured it was another celebrity scandal. Looking back, I wish it had been.

So I went downtown, and then I went back the next day, and the day after that. Front row, shouting, completely sure I belonged there. Right side of history. It's a big phrase to hand yourself, but that's what it felt like. Thousands of people had come outside because they couldn't stand what they'd seen either, and for once in my life I wasn't conflicted about anything.

Then the reports came out about the Black Lives Matter Global Network Foundation buying a house in Los Angeles for roughly six million dollars. It was all over the news.

I felt stupid.

That's embarrassing to admit, because nobody's real estate changes what happened to George Floyd. I didn't march because I'd reviewed a foundation's books. I marched because I watched a man die under another man's knee. I knew that. It didn't help.

The story still reached back and got its hands on the memory. The chanting, the certainty, how fast I'd put my whole heart into something without asking who was standing at the other end of it.

I started questioning my judgment. Then everyone else's, because apparently I like to be thorough.

That second part is the one I'm least proud of. A movement, an organization, and the stranger next to you downtown are three different things. They share a slogan, so in your head they become one thing, and when one of them lets you down you feel betrayed by all three. The person who offered me that loudspeaker probably had nothing to do with any foundation. In my memory they were just there, doing what I was doing. Making them answer for people they've never met is the same lazy thinking I was embarrassed about in the first place.

These days I'm slower. One clip, one source, one headline isn't enough anymore for me to throw a hundred percent of myself behind anything. I want a second look before I hand anyone my voice.

I honestly can't tell if that's wisdom or just what getting burned once does to a person. The guy who walked down from Balboa Park didn't wait for a second source, and he wasn't wrong about what he saw.

I'd probably still turn down the loudspeaker.
