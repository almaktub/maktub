On-Fire Ambulance

June 25, 2026

Sometimes you leave a relationship on good terms. You're both able to realize that, despite growing apart, without each other you wouldn't have become the person emotionally intelligent enough to know it was time to move on. A bittersweet moment of giving up something that gave you so much, in search of something more fulfilling.

This is not that story.

It was the worst relationship I've ever had in my life. You never forget your first and you never forget your worst. In hindsight I genuinely can't think of a single good memory from it. I know there were some. There must have been. You don't get into a relationship with someone you already hate, in the same way you don't get into an ambulance that's on fire, no matter how convincingly the paramedic tells you she's fully qualified and everything will be fine.

That was my ex: an on-fire ambulance. Telling me to get farther into the back because that's where the medicine is. The only reason I was burning was because I'd set the fire myself. It was all my fault.

I understand this is unfair. I'm writing about this relationship without giving her the chance to defend herself. I assure you that if I did, she would lie through her teeth and you would believe every single word of it. I probably would too. That's how good she was. Not since Blade Runner has something done such an incredible job of pretending to be a human being.

It wasn't physical violence. She was too smart for that. Physical bruises are too easy to see. But she managed to suck every single shred of happiness out of the core of who I am and reduce me to a dildo that had an accent and the same opinions she did. For someone who loves himself so greatly, I have to commend this woman for making me hate myself more than any hangover comedown anyone's ever had.

Emotional abuse is tough because it takes you ages to realize what's happening. Physical violence is horrific, but at least you know when you've been punched. There is blood. There are bruises. There is impact. Your brain goes "fucking ow" and lets you know something has gone terribly wrong.

Emotional abuse settles in slowly. Sometimes you're not even sure it happened. Imagine if your body didn't bruise and someone kept hitting you. Now imagine how easy it would be for your partner to convince you it never happened at all. That you were imagining things. Proving it is nearly impossible because you can't see it, and that's exactly why it works.

My friends and family had no idea how miserable I was. She kept up such a wonderful appearance in public. Beautiful, friendly, selfless, thoughtful. All the personality traits she had learned to mimic in order to get what she wanted. Then, the second no one else was watching, off came the mask.

If you blindfold someone and hold a slice of apple under his nose, not only are you an absolute weirdo, but if you then put a slice of onion in his mouth, his brain will convince him it's an apple. One trustworthy sense says apple, so it must be apple. When enough evidence points to something, no matter how ludicrous it is, you begin to believe it. So if enough people you love tell you someone is great, you believe them despite all evidence to the contrary. It makes you feel broken for not seeing what they see, when the problem is they can't see what you see.

Now, in her defense, I don't think she had any idea how much of an absolute cunt she was. Cunts don't know they're cunts. If they did, they wouldn't be cunts. They think other people are cunts. I don't think she was vindictive or that she planned any of it. I think it just came naturally to her. And I do respect raw talent.

Then again, maybe she did know. Some of it certainly seemed tactical. Making sure I never got enough sleep so I wouldn't have the energy to argue. Going through my sexual history and explaining why I was a gross, disgusting man who should be ashamed of who he was before she stepped in to fix him. Carefully turning me against anyone I seemed to love more than her: my best friends, my cousins, Real Madrid football club.

It's hard not to still hate her. I hate what she made me. I hate what she reduced me to. I hate the doubt she filled me with. I see the shit-covered footprints of her behavior across my life and probably will for a long time.

But mainly, it's hard not to hate myself. It's embarrassing to fall in love with someone who turns out to be a disaster. At the start you're besotted, convinced they're the one, telling all your friends how perfect they are. Then you realize the best meal you've ever had was literally shit on a plate. You've eaten shit in front of everyone you love and you just have to smile through it.

If you ever find yourself in something like this, there are two questions worth asking honestly:

Did I waste the last however many years of my life?

Do I waste the rest of it?

They sound simple. Answer them honestly and they'll tell you everything you need to do next.

The answer to the first one doesn't change. Whatever time you gave, you gave it. The answer to the second one is the only one you still have any say in.

I chose myself and my sanity. Don't for a second mistake that for indifference. I still feel guilty, and guilt is not a switch you turn off. But I'm slowly waking up to the fact that even the guilt was something she installed. She drilled it into me daily until it felt like mine. She had made sure to remind me, regularly, that if anything ever happened to her or to us, it would be because of me.

She passed away not long after.

I wasn't perfect. I never claimed to be. But it wasn't my fault. Goddamn it, it wasn't my fault. I just have to keep reminding myself of that.

I got out of the ambulance. She didn't. I don't know what to do with that.
